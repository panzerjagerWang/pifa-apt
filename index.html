Abstract

Attributing malware to the advanced persistent threat group that produced it guides the response to an intrusion. This attribution must stay reliable after commercial virtualization obfuscation has rewritten the code of a file. It must also resist an adversary who forges the metadata of the file. However, existing static attributors read byte or header features that such an adversary can rewrite. Their evaluations also shared executables or builds between training and testing.

Therefore, we propose the Perturbation-Invariant Forgery-Resistant Attribution (PIFA) model, which reads every field of the portable executable header with gradient-boosted trees. The model trains these trees on forged, false-flag, and edited copies of each executable, all labeled with the true group. A split on a manipulated field then separates samples of the same group, which drives the trees to use such a field only where it agrees with the unchanged fields. We also develop ObfusFormer, a multi-view Transformer that learns from each executable paired with its protected version. On unseen builds of nine groups, PIFA attributed VMProtect-protected executables with a macro-averaged F1 score of 91.2%. Its score stayed at 90.9% or higher under forged, false-flag, and edited headers. Without the copies, the score of the trees fell from 92.5% to 48.3% under forgery.
